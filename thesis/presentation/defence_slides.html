Purpose of the research

Find out which booked hours were really used — using the cameras a facility already owns.

No new hardware No GPU — one small PC A human makes every decision

The study looks at a network of 5-a-side football pitches, three kinds of scene — empty, active play, maintenance — and the facility’s own booking records.

Purpose03

Purpose

The purpose of this study is to find out which sold pitch hours were actually used, using cameras that are already installed for highlights.

Three constraints shape everything: no new hardware, no GPU, and the system never acts on its own. Say this plainly: the output is not a classification, it is a billing conversation. “This booking looks unused, and here are three frames from it.”

Overview — how it works04

How the research works

To achieve the purpose I built a five-stage pipeline and then evaluated it. Sampling one frame per camera per minute is the design decision that makes the whole thing fit on a CPU: about 99% less network traffic than decoding 20 to 30 live streams.

For the models I used frozen backbones — DINOv2, ConvNeXtV2, ViT — with a small trained head on top, because there are not enough labels to fine-tune anything. The important methodological choice is the last line: the split is grouped by venue, so nothing in the test set has a near-twin in training.

Overview — scenario05

The hypothetical scenario

Imagine it is Tuesday evening. The booking system says pitch 3 was sold for an hour and the staff marked it used. The cameras sampled sixteen frames and thirteen of them show an empty pitch under floodlights.

The system does not cancel the charge. It raises one advisory with three evidence photos, and a manager decides. In the code this is structural, not a promise: the only output type the decision layer can produce is an Advisory, and there is no code path that acts on its own.

Dataset06

Dataset

1,692 labelled frames, three classes, seven venue folds for cross-venue testing, and two cameras watching one pitch — that pair is what lets me measure onboarding cost later.

The last line matters more than it looks. Because the cameras are fixed and frames come every fifteen seconds, 98.5% of frames have a direct near-duplicate. A random split puts 37% of those near-duplicate pairs across the train/test line; a grouped split puts 0.8%. When I made the split honest, ConvNeXtV2 lost 0.49 macro-F1 — the score roughly halved. That is the cost of being honest, and I pay it in every number after this slide.

Dataset — the confound07

The confound — say this slowly

Across the recorded frames, daylight is 98% empty pitches and night is 99% football. So the sentence “night means play, day means not-play” is correct on 99.1% of the corpus.

That means nothing measured on this data can separate recognises an empty pitch from recognises the time of day. The two cells that would break the tie hold nine frames and six frames. This is not a footnote — it is the finding, and the next slides are its consequences.

The problem08

The problem, and the honest concession

This research addresses the problem that a facility sells pitch hours and has no verified record of which ones were used. Staff write it down; the records are unverified; three kinds of error follow.

Concede the obvious objection first, before an examiner raises it: a twenty-euro motion sensor is more robust in fog, in darkness, and against a dirty lens. But a sensor answers was something moving. The audit needs was this booking used, and here is the picture — and it has to tell a five-a-side match from a groundsman on a mower.

The problem — evidence09

The rule that never looks at the image

The clock rule predicts the majority class for the lighting condition. It reads no pixels at all. Across unseen venues it has perfect play-recall and a false-play rate fifteen times lower than the best backbone. It beats all three on both axes at once.

Expect the question: “so your backbones are worthless?” The answer is no — they are indistinguishable from a light meter on this dataset, which is a statement about the dataset, not about the models. Until the two missing cells are filled, no experiment here can separate an occupancy model from a light meter. Saying that clearly is the contribution.

One honesty note, if asked: this slide used to say the opposite. An earlier version reported that the rule collapsed across venues. That number came from a label error — a brightness threshold filed 216 frames of floodlit night football as daylight. Corrected, the rule wins.

The problem — the reversal10

The clip that reversed the ranking

This is the cell the corpus does not have: a floodlit pitch at night with nobody on it. Four minutes of it, scored minute by minute.

The clock rule calls all sixteen minutes active play — it is wrong on every single one. The probe alone sits at 0.38. The deployed path — probe plus pitch boundary plus motion gate plus person gate — is at zero, and gets all thirteen empty minutes right.

The point to land: the benchmark had 1,692 frames and the clip had a few hundred. The clip won, because it contained the case the benchmark was missing. More data of the same kind would not have found this.

The searches — preprocessing12

The preprocessing search

Eighty-eight runs over crop, undistort, gamma, saturation, sharpening, denoise and CLAHE, greedily, four rounds. It produced a clear winner that scores a perfect 1.000 on the cross-venue folds.

Then I measured the resolution of the search itself, and that is the slide. One frame moving in the smallest venue fold shifts the headline by 0.0119. The bootstrap interval over the seven folds is 0.092 wide at the median configuration. Almost every difference the search ranked on is smaller than its own error bar. So the ranking is real arithmetic on unreal precision.

The bonus line is the useful one for the thesis story: the proposal explicitly predicted CLAHE would help with floodlight glare. Measured honestly, it costs 0.27 macro-F1. The plan was wrong and the measurement said so.

The searches — prompts13

The prompt search

375 prompt sets, no labels used at all. The span from worst to best wording is 0.726 macro-F1. Choosing between the three trained backbones — the decision the whole model chapter is about — moves the score by 0.082. So how you write the sentence matters about nine times more than which model you pick.

Two guards on reading that as a win. First, only 22.9% of prompt sets beat the best trained probe, and the median one loses — so “zero-shot works” is only true if you already know which wording to use, which needs labels. Second, the winner’s lead of 0.447 over a pre-declared prompt is measured on the very folds it was selected from. That is selection bias as a number, and it is disclosed in the pre-registration as an undeclared search family.

Augmentation14

Augmentation — the retraction

The idea is sound and standard: if the new camera is brighter or darker than the training one, augment the training images across brightness and gamma so the model stops caring. On the draw I ran first, it worked beautifully — empty-pitch recall on a camera the model had never seen went from zero to 0.687, with no labels from that camera at all. That went into the write-up as a finding.

Then I ran the identical experiment four more times, changing nothing but the random seed. 0.414, 0.351, 0.350, 0.348. The published 0.855 is the maximum of five, not a typical value. Four of the five draws are worse than using no augmentation at all, and the standard deviation is 0.221 on a metric bounded between zero and one.

Be precise about what was retracted: the row in the results file is unchanged and still reproduces exactly. What was withdrawn is the claim about the method. It is now restated as a claim about that one draw, and it is only ever quoted next to the spread.

The lesson

The result did not.

It is the model answering “playing” to every single frame. A trivial predictor, wearing a number.

A test that pins the value cannot catch this

The row was reproducible. That is exactly why the check had to be a re-draw, not a re-run.

So: five draws, always

Every headline now runs five times. We ran it on our own best result first.

Augmentation — the lesson15

Why this slide exists

Three things worth saying out loud here.

First, 0.3479 kept recurring across presets that share nothing else. When I added a column counting how many frames were predicted empty, the answer was zero every time. That score is not a measurement of augmentation — it is the macro-F1 of answering “active play” to everything.

Second, and this is the methodological point: a unit test that fixes the published value would have passed forever. The artefact was reproducible. Only re-drawing the randomness exposed it.

Third, this is now standing practice, and I applied it to my own strongest result — the temporal model — before anyone asked. Volunteering that is the point; an examiner trusts a candidate who ran the check on their own headline.

The answer16

How the research provides a solution

Three interventions, each measured, and the common thread is that none of them is a bigger network.

One: putting cheap geometric and motion rules in front of the model. False alarms on empty pitches drop from 0.617 to 0.012. Counting every kind of wrong verdict, not just play-shaped ones, error falls from 1.00 to 0.11.

Two: generating the cell the data does not have. Thirty-one synthetic empty-pitch frames take cross-venue false alarms from 0.768 to 0.024 while raising play-recall.

Three: the deployment answer. A model trained on one camera scores 0.36 to 0.51 on the second camera watching the same pitch. One labelled frame from the new camera takes every backbone to about 0.98. And the control that matters: from five frames, training on those five alone matches training on those five plus 775 from the source camera. State the weaker, true version — what buys the accuracy is having any labels from the new camera.

Limits

What this study cannot claim

Named first, because an examiner will find them anyway.

01

Empty pitches exist at one venue only

So a cross-venue three-class test is impossible here.

02

243 empty frames are 3 scenes

The real sample is scenes, not frames. Recounted that way, several results stop being significant.

03

Nothing has run on the target hardware

Every speed number comes from a laptop.

What would fix it

Empty-pitch footage at a second venue  ·  30 real booked slots  ·  5 labelled frames per camera

Limits17

Limits, and what would change them

Three limits, stated before anyone has to ask.

On the sample size point, be concrete: 243 held-out empty frames sound like a lot, but they are three distinct scenes. Six comparisons that were significant to p equals 8 times ten to the minus 53 did not survive being recounted that way. I also report, beside every p-value, the smallest p the design could have produced — over seven folds that is 0.0156. “Not significant” and “the design could not have produced significance” are different statements.

Close on the fix, not the apology: these are a data-access problem with a known and inexpensive solution, not a methodological one. Empty-pitch footage at a second venue fixes the confound, the external-validity limit and the scope reduction at once — and it is one conversation with the facility.

Summary

Four sentences

  • Built. Sample one frame a minute, classify it, check it, turn an hour into a verdict, compare it to the booking — on a CPU.
  • Found. On this data a clock beats a neural network. That is a fact about the dataset, not about the networks.
  • Fixed. Rules in the path, generated empty frames, and five labelled frames per new camera — all measured, all cheap.
  • Learned. A result that reproduces is not a result that repeats. Every headline now runs five draws, including our own.

No protocol compensates for a cell the data never fills.

Summary18

Summary

In summary, this study investigated whether existing cameras can verify which booked pitch hours were actually used, on hardware a facility already owns.

The findings indicate that the system works end to end, and that the evaluation is the contribution: on this corpus, lighting and occupancy are confounded so tightly that a rule reading only the clock outperforms three modern backbones — and one short clip containing the missing case reverses that ranking completely.

These findings contribute a set of evaluation practices — trivial baselines in every protocol, grouped splits, re-drawing randomness rather than re-running it, and verifying each guard by deliberately breaking what it guards — and give the facility a concrete, inexpensive recipe for onboarding a new camera.

Last sentence, delivered slowly: no protocol compensates for a cell the data never fills.

Closing, and the reserve questions

Thank you for your time and attention. I welcome any questions, comments, suggestions or feedback you may have.

The chips are the six questions most likely to come. Short answers: the colour histogram scores 0.9616 on the leaky split and is indistinguishable from ConvNeXtV2 on the 62 distinct scenes. The novelty is tested against four tuned baselines including an HMM, with a saturation caveat enforced in code. Multiple comparisons are Holm-corrected within declared families, with the undeclared ones disclosed as amendments. Ethics: per-field reporting only, and the decision layer can structurally only advise. Reproduction: one pipeline re-derives every claim from its artefact on every run. And the human ceiling — say plainly that no inter-annotator figure exists. It is a real gap. Practise saying “we do not know” without flinching.
