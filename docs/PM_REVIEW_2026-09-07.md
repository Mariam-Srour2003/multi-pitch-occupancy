## The one-paragraph version

This project is in unusually good health. The engineering discipline is real: leakage is
quantified rather than asserted, hypotheses are pre-registered, negative results are reported,
and three separate findings were self-corrected before this review arrived. **The problems are
not sloppiness — they are the drift you get when a fast-moving project's *documents* fall behind
its *results*.** Four defects would have changed a reported number or invalidated a guarantee;
all four are now fixed. Two milestone gates had quietly become impossible to pass and have been
re-cut. The single largest remaining risk is not scientific: 4.2 GB of irreplaceable footage
still exists in one place.

## Plan changes made

### 1. Two milestone gates had become unpassable. Re-cut.

Decision 0.3 closed the footage request; **nobody re-cut the gates that depended on it.**
M2 required "dataset balanced across classes/conditions/venues + ≥30 real slots"; M4 required
STAN "ablated with significance". Two real slots exist, C3 has 6 frames, maintenance has 0,
EMPTY exists at exactly one venue, and no further footage is coming — so **both criteria became
impossible the moment 0.3 was taken**, and WP5-T8's own hard gate already forbids reporting STAN
as a headline below 30 slots.

A gate that cannot be passed is not a gate; it is a permanent red row that teaches everyone to
ignore the table, and it hides the fact that the rest of the milestone was met. Both are re-cut
against the data that exists, **originals struck through so the reduction is visible**, each
saying what would restore it. No research question changes and no contribution is dropped.

### 2. WP5's order was pointing at the wrong module first. Swapped — on measured evidence.

STAN (5.A, labelled *"Core tier — do this one first"*) is the **only** WP5 module that is
data-blocked: it is a slot classifier with a real test set of n = 2. Gated fusion (5.B, demoted
to *"Target tier"*) is frame-level and blocked by **nothing** — all three backbone caches cover
all 1,692 frames and the gate's cheap statistics are already cached. It can be fitted in seconds.

**So 5.B now carries the M4 gate.** The headroom was measured, not assumed, from
`h3_with_false_play.csv`: DINOv2 leads on the unweighted fold mean (0.9297) but is **strictly
beaten on three of seven venue folds**, and a per-fold oracle reaches **0.9603 — +3.1 points**.
There is real complementarity.

Two caveats are written into the plan beside it, because without them this becomes a fishing
trip: (a) on the *other* axis DINOv2 dominates outright (false-play 0.309 vs ConvNeXtV2's 0.992),
so the ablation must report both axes, never recall alone; (b) the gate reads cheap image
statistics, which at `venue_01` are nearly a day/night indicator, which is nearly the class
label — so it risks re-learning the clock rule, and must be ablated against a lighting-only gate.
+3.1 is an *oracle* ceiling, so **a clean negative result is a likely and acceptable outcome** —
provided WP5-T9's logit-average baseline runs first, which is why it was promoted to the front.

### 3. `thesis/mvt.md` written — the minimum viable thesis.

Asked for in the capacity check and never written; with three of seven RQs data-blocked it is
now the most valuable planning artefact in the project. Four load-bearing items, **all already
in hand**, and an explicit list of what the floor does *not* require. Its conclusion: the one
genuine hole in the floor is the **unwritten labelling protocol** (WP1-T2) — 1,692 labels rest on
a definition that exists only in one person's head, and it is the M1 artefact that was due in
week 4. It is a writing task and it should come before anything in WP5.

### 4. Pre-registration amendments A1–A7 — the log was empty while six hypotheses had moved.

This is the document an examiner compares against the write-up, and its own rules say *every
hypothesis is reported* and *the family is declared with the hypothesis*. Both had drifted:

- **H2 is refuted at its pre-registered threshold** (and confirmed in a narrower form). Recorded
  as missed rather than quietly revised.
- **H1's finding stands; its wording and p-value do not** — on 62 distinct scenes DINOv2 and a
  colour histogram both score 1.0000, so the claim becomes *"the protocol cannot tell them
  apart."*
- **H4, H5 and H6 appear nowhere outside the pre-registration** — not in the log, the RQ matrix,
  or the task list. Now declared *outstanding* with what each needs, rather than silently missing.
- **451 evaluations ran outside the declared family structure** (76-config preprocessing search,
  375-row prompt search). Disclosed, with the honest treatment: searched configurations are
  **selected, not tested**, and a search-winning delta is never a significance result.

### 5. Risk register recalibrated.

Most triggers were date-locked on week numbers, and several waited on answers to a request that
0.3 closed — so they could never fire. Resolved risks are now closed (a register that never
closes anything is not being used), and the live ones re-triggered on something observable.

## Findings reported but not acted on

A parallel audit raised 45 candidate findings across ten dimensions, each then attacked by
independent verifiers instructed to refute it — one checking the claim against the code as
written, one checking whether the repo already documented it. Of 36 verdicts returned before
this review was written, **16 refuted**. Several findings described code that does not exist in
the working tree (they were written against an older commit); several had accurate mechanics but
a consequence that did not follow, or that the log already caveated. That filter is why the list
above is short — and it is also why everything in the *Fixed* table was independently reproduced
here before being touched, rather than taken on an agent's word.

Two survived verification, and they turned out to be **one defect with two faces** — the same
root cause, and both are now fixed rather than deferred as this section first said:

- **`bootstrap_metric_ci`'s macro-F1 estimand changed between resamples**, because `evaluate()`
  was asked to drop zero-support classes *inside* the bootstrap. C3 has support **1** in the
  random split's test set and entered **63.4%** of resamples, so two thirds averaged three
  classes and one third averaged two.
- **H1's headline comparison therefore put a 3-class macro-F1 against a 2-class one**, which is
  the same problem seen from the results side.

Fixing it moved a headline number, and moved it **against us being modest**: H1's drop from the
leaky to the honest protocol was reported as −0.159 macro-F1 for ConvNeXtV2, and on a consistent
metric it is **−0.490 — about 3.1× larger**. Leakage does not shave points off this benchmark,
it halves the score, which is a far cleaner statement of the thesis's central result. Affected
intervals also narrowed ~15×. Every grouped-split number, all of H2's grouped conclusions, and
all of H3 are untouched.

One claim weakened and was restated rather than dropped: the **ranking inversion stands as a
statement about rank** — ViT is still the model a reader of the pilot's protocol selects — but
its magnitude does not. ViT's apparent 0.339 lead under the leaky protocol came almost entirely
from correctly classifying that single C3 frame; on equal footing the lead is **0.006**. Quote
the inversion as a reversal of ordering, never as a large leaky-protocol margin.

**And fixing it exposed a third defect.** Regenerating `figs/ranking_inversion.png` changed
nothing — because `fig_ranking_inversion()` **hardcoded both the ranks and the printed scores**
and never read the CSV. It kept displaying the old 0.657 after the value became 0.988: the most
quoted figure in the thesis contradicting its own source table, while the reproduction stage
reported success and its docstring claimed the figures were "regenerated from the CSVs". Both
are derived from the data now, and so is the subtitle, which asserted ViT was "last under the
honest one" — true of the old numbers, and now "tied 2nd".

That is the **second** instance in one review of a number appearing in an output without being
computed from the data it describes (the fingerprint AUC column was the first). Two in a day is
enough to call it the pattern to watch for in this repo.

## Where the project actually stands

**The strongest thing here is not the accuracy numbers — it is the evaluation methodology**, and
that contribution is already complete and survives every data limit below it. The
decision-reversal result (a reader following the pilot's protocol selects the *weakest*
generaliser) is a genuine, defensible, publishable finding.

The gap is the ordinary one for a project moving this fast: **WP0/WP3/WP4 are deep, and WP1 is
almost untouched.** The M1 gate — protocol, taxonomy and evaluation design approved — was due in
week 4 and its central artefact does not exist. There is no related-work skeleton, no
alternative-solutions page (*"why not a €20 motion sensor?"* is the most dangerous question you
can be asked and the cheapest to answer in advance), and no supervisor sign-off on any gate.

**The next three things, in order:**

1. **Back up `data/`.** Today. One hour.
2. **Write the labelling protocol** (WP1-T2). It is the only hole in the minimum viable thesis.
3. **Run WP5-T9** — the logit-average baseline, ~20 lines on cached features. It decides whether
   6 weeks of gated-fusion work is a contribution or a documented negative result. Find out
   cheaply, first.
