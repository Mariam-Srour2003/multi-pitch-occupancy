## The pattern worth naming: guards that were not guarding

Five, found separately, all reading in code review and in prose exactly like working guards.

| | The guard | Why it did nothing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The final-test-set lock** | Consulted through a cwd-relative path, and a missing file returned an empty set. From any other directory both locked venues — 114 frames — silently joined training. *(day one)* |
| 2 | **The `processor_geometry` cache fingerprint** | `protocol.md` and the experiment log both stated the flag was part of the fingerprint. It was in neither the fingerprint nor `build_cache`'s signature — the alternative convention could not be produced at all, and had it been, both conventions would have collided on one cache key *and* one filename. |
| 3 | **The two confidence thresholds** | `Thresholds.review_below_confidence` and `reconcile(min_confidence=…)` both default to `0.0`. `conf < 0.0` is never true, no non-test caller raises either, and `end_to_end_slots.py` passes `0.0` explicitly. So "low-confidence slots are downgraded before any rule runs" describes a wired path, not a protection. |
| 4 | **The search viewer's false-play safeguard** | Lived in `thesis_site._search_summary()`, called by **one test and nothing else**. `page()` never included it. A test certified a safeguard no reader ever saw, while the panel that *was* rendered ranked by recall alone and served 45 un-rescored entries — carrying a placeholder `0.0000` — as clean top results. |
| 5 | **`read_split`'s lock re-application, and `check_split` on materialised splits** | The round-trip lost `group_key`, leaving a placeholder that is not a manifest field — so `check_split`, the leakage validation the module exists for, raised `AttributeError` on *every* split read from disk. And `read_split` never re-applied the final-venue lock. |

All five are fixed, each with tests. The one that generalises is **#4**: the difference between
testing a function and testing the page. Its replacement test asserts that what
`_search_summary()` produces actually appears in the **served HTML** — which is what would
have caught it.

A sixth, adjacent: the module docstring of `splits.py` claimed "splits are materialised, never
re-randomised, referenced by name". `write_split`/`read_split` have **no callers outside the
tests** and `results/splits/` holds only the lock file. Not fixed by fiat — adopting
materialisation is a retrofit across ~20 scripts and dropping the claim is nearly free, so
WP0-T4 now carries both options and the evidence. The machinery works either way now.

## Two of day one's conclusions were overturned

### 1. H1's effect was understated by a factor of three, not overstated

`bootstrap_metric_ci` called `evaluate()` *inside* the bootstrap, so the class set was
re-derived per resample. C3 has support **1** in the random split's test set and appears in
**63.4%** of resamples — two thirds averaged three classes, one third averaged two, and one
interval covered both estimands.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| ConvNeXtV2, random split | 0.657 | **0.988** |
| H1's leakage drop | −0.159 | **−0.490** (≈3.1× larger) |
| CI width, affected rows | 0.348 | **0.023** (~15× narrower) |
| Grouped split, every model | — | **unchanged** |

Leakage does not shave points off this benchmark, it **halves the score** — a cleaner
statement of the central methodological result. One claim weakened and was restated rather
than dropped: the ranking inversion stands as a fact about **rank**, but ViT's apparent 0.339
lead under the leaky protocol came almost entirely from the single C3 frame it got right. On
equal footing that lead is **0.006**.

Fixing it exposed a third defect: `fig_ranking_inversion()` **hardcoded both ranks and printed
scores** and never read the CSV, so the most-quoted figure in the thesis contradicted its own
source table while the reproduction stage reported success.

**And my first version of the fix was wrong.** It restricted *accuracy* to the evaluable
subset too, which moved the published accuracies by up to 0.0025 —
`effective_sample_audit.py` reproduces them to 5e-4 and refused to run. It was right to. The
estimand problem belongs to the macro *average over classes*; accuracy has no such
difficulty. Worth noting what caught it: a reproduce-before-extending guard in a different
file, written for another purpose, with no test covering the interaction.

### 2. The production recommendation is back in question, and the fusion verdict was refuted

The geometry probe (WP3-T3) was meant to settle a narrow convention question. It did — **keep
the processor's geometry**, against what `protocol.md` leaned toward — and then produced
something much larger.

Every published cross-venue number comes from caches built by handing **raw frames** to the HF
processor, which resizes a 1920×1080 frame shortest-edge to 256 and centre-crops 224 — keeping
roughly the **middle half** of the pitch. `build_cache` never applies `preprocess.py` at all;
its `preproc` argument is a dict of *labels for the fingerprint* and has never driven a
transform. So WP3-T2's letterbox, ROI masking, CLAHE and the rest of the ten searched switches
are absent from every cached feature the headline experiments read.

Apply the letterbox instead:

| backbone | balanced, raw | balanced, letterboxed |
|---|---|---|
| ConvNeXtV2 | −0.081 | **+0.964** |
| DINOv2 | +0.621 | +0.729 |
| ViT | +0.034 | −0.059 |

**ConvNeXtV2's false-play of 0.9918 — the number behind "says PLAY to almost everything", and
part of why the pick moved to DINOv2 — is 0.0206 letterboxed**, with recall rising to 0.9841.
It then leads on *both* axes and is the fastest of the three.

That also refuted day one's WP5-T9 finding. I had concluded *blending is disqualified* because
every ensemble scored 1.0000 false-play. On letterboxed caches the two-model ensemble scores
**0.0288** at **1.0000** recall — the best balanced score measured here. Blending was never the
problem; blending *a model shown a central strip of the pitch* was. The other half strengthened:
a parameter-free average now captures **100%** of the oracle headroom (was 74%), so **WP5-T2's
answer is unchanged and its reasoning is now the opposite** — not "blending is disqualified, so
a gate must be a hard router" but "the ensemble is already at the ceiling, so a gate has no
room". That is the cleaner negative result.

**Neither the recommendation nor the input path was changed on this evidence**, and the plan
says why: the false-play column is 243 frames amounting to **three to ten distinct scenes**,
with no CIs, one seed and no paired test. The recall axis is also saturated — the oracle is
1.0000 and the headroom was only +0.0159 — so "100% of it" is a smaller claim than it sounds.

## Other fixes

- **A camera gap moved the clock in transition evidence.** `find_transitions` returned a list
  *position* in a field named `minute`; the evidence selector looked it up in a dict keyed by
  real minute. On a slot losing its first ten minutes, evidence for a change at minute 20 came
  back as minutes 10 and 12 — two frames instead of three, **both still showing the old
  state**. Fixed on all three surfaces that read the field as a time.
- **H4 reported** — the first of three pre-registered hypotheses never reported. Refuted as a
  whole: the equivalence is exact with a **zero-width** interval because ConvNeXtV2 and ViT
  emit *identical* predictions (ACTIVE_PLAY for all 907 frames, EMPTY zero times), and the
  speed clause fails on the pre-registration's own stated condition (**1.83×** on the
  20-camera concurrent median against ≥2× required). Its decision rule — "a null result
  confirms H4" — was disowned as the absence-of-evidence error and replaced with an interval
  test against a margin declared in advance.
- **The Models tab showed no uncertainty** while `collect()` read the very CSVs carrying
  `ci_low`/`ci_high` and dropped the columns — on the page that makes the production
  recommendation, against standing rule 3. The intervals change what it says: DINOv2's
  overlaps both rivals', so the page now names them and states the lead **is not established
  by the intervals**.
- **Signed numbers displayed their sign as literal entity text** (`&minus;0.2000`) on the
  Findings tab, six times.
- **The labelling protocol was written** — `thesis/labelling_protocol.md`, the M1 artefact due
  in week 4 and the one hole `mvt.md` identified. It needs supervisor sign-off and two client
  answers, which are not ours.
